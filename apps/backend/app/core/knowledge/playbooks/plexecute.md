# Plexecute content playbook

Richard's standing rules for every piece of Plexecute content JAI makes: videos, carousels, posts, scripts. Read this
before anything else on a content mission. A brief can add to it; only Richard can change it.

## What Plexecute is

Plexecute is personal growth and development, self-improvement, accountability, and planning and execution, rolled
up into one app, with an AI coach that helps with anything a person is working on: careers, confidence, personal
development, learning new skills, wellness, fitness, nutrition, finance. The topics are effectively endless.

## Organic content, not ads

- Content is organic: it is about the topics Plexecute covers and is worth watching for its own sake. It teaches,
  moves or entertains; it does not sell.
- No call to action: no waiting list, no plexecute.co.uk, no "join", "sign up", "download" or end card. Being posted
  on Plexecute's own account is the branding.
- Only a brief that says "ad" gets an ad, with a call to action.

## Research before creating, every time

1. Start from what Plexecute is (above) and find the accounts in this space that are winning now: search the themes
   (self-improvement, discipline, productivity, accountability, planning, habits, and the coach's topics) on YouTube
   Shorts and TikTok, and keep the accounts that keep appearing with high views as the competitor watchlist.
2. Rank their recent videos by outlier score: views divided by that creator's median views. An outlier beat its own
   creator's normal; that is a format or hook that works on its merits, not because the account is big.
3. Build from the outliers: name the outlier(s) the piece adapts and why (hook, structure, pacing, format), then
   change the topic, the words and the visuals. Never copy a video, a line or a clip.
4. Formats are few, topics are endless: rotate Plexecute's topics through the formats that are winning now.

Never generic: no stock motivational quotes, no stock stories, no "hustle" clichés. Every piece traces back to a
real outlier and a real Plexecute topic.

## Voice and look

- Narration is a natural human voice or no voice. Never a robotic text-to-speech voice in a finished piece.
- No music.
- Choose the voice and its energy to fit the piece and the outliers it adapts: calm and reflective for a quote
  loop, punchy and fast for a hook-led reel.
- Every piece has a different subject, story and look from the ones before it.
- Real footage, images and voices are licensed for commercial use, with each one's source and licence saved
  beside the piece.

## Care with health and money

Fitness, nutrition, wellness and finance content stays general or sourced: habits, planning, mindset and
consistency are fine; specific diets, doses, medical claims or investment calls are out unless a credible source
backs them, named in the piece's notes.

## Richard gives the story

When a brief gives the story, that is the story. The research decides the format, hook and pacing that carry it.

## Series

A series keeps one look and one format across its episodes; each episode takes a different Plexecute topic.

### The black stick-figure series

- Look: a plain black background, white stick figures and simple white line drawings, one accent colour at most.
  Nothing else on screen: no stock footage, no photos, no gradients, no logo.
- One mechanism per episode: a single idea about how people grow, plan or follow through, shown as it happens
  (a habit compounding, a plan collapsing, a streak breaking and restarting), never a quote read out.
- Data on screen: counters, tallies, timelines or simple charts that make the mechanism visible, every number
  either illustrative and labelled as such, or sourced in the episode's notes.
- Hook in the first 2 seconds: the figure is already in the situation; no title card, no intro.
- Sound: no voice, or a natural human voice that fits the episode. No music.
- 20 to 45 seconds, 9:16, every frame readable on a phone.
- No call to action and no end card.
- Each episode names, in its notes, the outlier videos its hook and pacing adapt.
